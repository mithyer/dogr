Made for Herdr

A phone for the agents Herdr keeps running.

Herdr keeps your coding agents running in terminals on your computer. Dogr is the app that stays in touch with them when you are away from the desk.

Works with both

Dogr connects to Herdr TUI and to herdr-core. Pick the one you use when you add a computer.

The same words

Needs you, working, done, idle. Spaces and panes. Dogr reads the same theme files as the Herdr desktop app, so it can look like home.

Independent

Dogr is not made by the Herdr team. It is a fan’s project, built because Herdr is worth having in your pocket. Get Herdr at herdr.dev.

Experience

How it feels comes first.

The first rule of Dogr is how it feels. Every choice below is there because it saves you a tap, a wait, or a moment of “where was I?”.

Swipe, don’t dig.

Machines, spaces and the terminal are three panels side by side. One swipe moves between them, with your thumb. Close the app and open it tomorrow: it opens where you left it.

  • On iPad the panels stand next to each other.
  • In landscape you get all three at once.

Answer with a tap.

When an agent asks something (a permission, a choice, a step in a wizard) the question shows up as buttons. Tap the answer. No hunting for the right key.

When you do need keys, the bar is there: Esc, Tab, Ctrl and the arrows.

Read the conversation, not only the terminal.

A conversation view shows what the agent said and did: tables, code and tool calls, laid out to be read on a phone. Older messages load as you scroll up, and the conversation stays on your phone, so you pick up where you stopped.

Calm when the network is not.

If the connection drops, Dogr keeps the last screen in front of you and tries again, slowly, instead of going blank and hammering the link. One loading mark per area, never a pile of spinners.

Yours to adjust.

  • 800+ built-in themes, every Ghostty theme included, plus the Herdr theme file format.
  • Text size from 80% to 140%.
  • iPhone and iPad, portrait and landscape.
  • 12 languages.

Connect

Reach your computer your way.

No account, no sign-in. Pick the route that fits your setup. One command on the computer sets it up and shows a QR code to scan. Or give the app an SSH login and it runs that command for you; the password is used once and not saved.

Tailscale

Already use it? Dogr connects over your tailnet.

Tailcat

No Tailscale? An encrypted link between your phone and your computer, no account.

Iroh

Another encrypted link over a different relay network, for when Tailcat can’t connect. You can run your own relay.

Your own proxy

Already put the computer behind an HTTPS reverse proxy? Point Dogr at its address.

Same Wi-Fi

At home, connect over the local network.

Honest note: on the same network it is fast. Away from home, a route that goes through a relay can be slower than you would like.

Get started

Three steps.

Herdr on your computer

Dogr needs Herdr (TUI or herdr-core) running on a Mac or Linux computer. Get it at herdr.dev.

One command

Run it in a terminal on that computer. It sets up what Dogr needs and shows a QR code.

Scan in the app

Open Dogr, add a computer, scan the code. Your agents appear.

curl -fsSL https://dogr.dev/setup.sh | sh

The script says what it is about to do and asks once before it changes anything. You can read it first; options and how to undo everything are in the README. The app also shows the exact command for the route you pick.

I love Herdr. It changed how I work with coding agents.

What I wanted most was a way to look after them from my phone that feels as good as Herdr does on the computer. Dogr is my try at that.

It is new, and there is a lot left to polish. For now I am putting everything into making it feel right on iPhone and iPad; other platforms can wait until the features and the way it behaves have settled. The best way to make it better is to hear from you.
